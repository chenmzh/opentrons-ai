## Intended contribution

An exploratory case study of human–AI co-development, with an explicit
architecture, attributed human decisions, acoustic-feedback episodes, and
bounded physical measurements. The operator contributed the research goals,
microphone setup, phrase-level reversal idea, and whole-score precomposition
request. The agent implemented and interpreted experiments through tools.

Use “acoustic feedback between experimental rounds” rather than “real-time
autonomous pitch control.” The quantitative feedback supplement compares a
fixed perturbed mapping with a conventional measurement-based update. It does
not compare LLMs or establish that the agent was necessary for numerical fitting.

## Claims requiring more evidence

- Incremental AI benefit over a conventional calibration method: would need
  independently restarted agent and baseline conditions with equivalent prior
  information, measurement access, physical trial budgets, and frozen scoring.
- Reduction of human labor or increased autonomy: would need prospectively
  recorded human interventions, time, and explicit definitions of assistance.
- General pitch perception or full chromatic range: would need independent
  pitch evaluation and listening evidence beyond the present harmonic methods.
- Transfer to other robots: would need new-device data; cross-day testing of
  this device does not establish such transfer.

These are limitations of stronger claims, not results silently assumed in the
current case-study manuscript. A complete model/configuration/prompt record is
not available for the original development session. The selected related work
is a contextual comparison, not a claim to have exhausted all prior art.
